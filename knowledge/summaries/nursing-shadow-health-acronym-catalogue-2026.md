# Nursing & Shadow Health acronym catalogue — 2026

## Purpose

This is a **living, role-specific catalogue** for product work on Shadow Health. It is not intended to become a dictionary of every bedside abbreviation in medicine.

The useful boundary is: **will this term plausibly appear in a conversation about nursing education, simulation, assessment, accreditation, clinical judgment, graduate nursing, educational technology, customer implementation, or the Shadow Health / HESI portfolio?** If yes, it belongs here. If it is primarily a medication, disease, lab, or unit-specific clinical abbreviation, it belongs in a separate clinical-content reference.

### Priority

- **P0 — fluent:** know it without stopping to translate it.
- **P1 — recognition:** understand it immediately in a meeting or document.
- **P2 — lookup:** specialist/contextual vocabulary; know roughly what domain it belongs to and look it up when needed.

### Shadow Health scoring distinction to keep straight

**DCE score ≠ SPI.** Public support documentation treats the DCE score as the percentage commonly used for grading/effort, while SPI and proficiency reporting characterize performance on underlying activities or clinical-reasoning dimensions. If someone says “score,” ask which signal they mean before drawing product conclusions.

## What not to memorize yet

Do **not** spend early-role attention memorizing hundreds of medication abbreviations, lab abbreviations, disease acronyms, device names, or unit-specific shorthand. That would create the feeling of domain fluency without the part that actually matters for product judgment.

For this role, deeper fluency should come first in:

1. **program architecture:** ADN, BSN, ABSN, MSN, DNP, APRN;
2. **authority structure:** BON, NCSBN, AACN, CCNE, ACEN, NLN;
3. **learning and assessment:** CBE, NCJMM, NGN, OSCE, QSEN, SBE;
4. **Shadow Health language:** DCE, DSP, SPI, HESI;
5. **implementation:** LMS, LTI, SIS, SSO, FERPA, WCAG;
6. **simulation ecosystem:** INACSL, HSSOBP, SSH, SP, CHSE/CHSOS.

The clinical shorthand should then be learned **in the context of the scenarios and competencies Shadow Health actually teaches**, rather than as an abstract list.

## Maintenance rule

Whenever an unfamiliar acronym appears in an internal document, customer call, interview, roadmap, support ticket, or product UI:

- add the acronym and its spelled-out form;
- label its domain and P0/P1/P2 priority;
- add a collision note if the same acronym has another common meaning;
- link to a public or internal source where possible;
- prefer the current official organization/product terminology over historical expansions;
- if it is Elsevier-internal terminology, label it explicitly as internal after verification;
- periodically promote repeated P2 terms to P1 and repeated P1 terms to P0 based on observed use.

This catalogue should become a record of the **language actually used around the product**, not a static nursing glossary.
