# Shadow Health product and audience map — 2026

## The job Shadow Health performs

The core product gives a healthcare learner a patient encounter that is **safe, repeatable, standardized, observable, and scoreable**. The learner can fail without harming a patient, repeat practice, receive immediate feedback, and encounter conditions that might not reliably appear in a clinical placement.

The product is best understood as a bridge between knowing material and performing patient-facing judgment.

## Core Digital Clinical Experience

A typical DCE can involve:

1. reviewing the case/patient context;
2. interviewing the Digital Standardized Patient using natural language;
3. gathering subjective data;
4. performing virtual physical examination actions and gathering objective data;
5. responding with education and empathy;
6. reviewing or creating documentation;
7. processing findings and making care/diagnostic decisions;
8. reflecting on the encounter;
9. receiving detailed feedback, DCE/SPI scoring, and proficiency information.

The **Conversation Engine** is historically central: students type their own questions/statements rather than choosing only from a fixed multiple-choice dialogue tree. Elsevier currently describes it as patented and AI-enabled/NLP-based.

## Product breadth

Current Elsevier pages describe:

- **80+ Digital Standardized Patients** across diverse demographics and conditions;
- **11 undergraduate nursing collections**, spanning much of the prelicensure curriculum;
- **8 graduate collections**, spanning advanced assessment, diagnostics, pharmacology, pathophysiology, and primary-care specialties;
- current use by **700,000+ nursing students**, according to Elsevier's product marketing, though the exact period/active-user definition is not public.

## Measurement layer

Shadow Health measures more than completion.

- **DCE Score** is the normalized percentage Elsevier recommends for grading.
- **SPI** is the underlying clinical-judgment performance instrument for applicable encounters.
- **Practice Readiness Dashboard** gives faculty course-, student-, assignment-, engagement-, and category-level views.
- Learners can review their transcript and detailed evidence of where they did or did not collect information, communicate, document, or reason effectively.

The important product idea is therefore **practice → trace → feedback → remediation**, not simply “play a simulation.”

## 2026 expansion: Shadow Health Lab + VR

Shadow Health Lab broadens the modality stack:

- 165+ lab-based simulations;
- 30 VR encounters;
- one multi-patient prioritization/coordination scenario;
- prebriefing, facilitation and debriefing support;
- SimChart documentation;
- Osmosis learning resources;
- implementation guides, remediation, reporting and moulage resources.

This brings Shadow Health closer to the physical simulation center and lets Elsevier pitch one brand across screen simulation, hands-on lab work and VR.

## Learner audiences

### Undergraduate/prelicensure nursing

The largest and clearest audience. The product supports skill development from fundamentals and health assessment through specialty and med-surg contexts and Next Generation NCLEX clinical-judgment preparation.

### Graduate/advanced-practice nursing

More diagnostic emphasis: advanced assessment, diagnostics, pathophysiology, pharmacology, adult/pediatric/women's/mental-health primary care.

### Novice/new-graduate nurses

Elsevier maintains positioning for healthcare organizations that need new nurses to strengthen reasoning and therapeutic communication during transition to practice.

## Decision-makers and implementers

The buyer is not the learner alone. Public Elsevier marketing targets:

- deans and nursing-program directors;
- course faculty;
- simulation directors/lab leaders;
- institutional purchasing/education leadership;
- in workforce contexts, nurse educators and leaders.

Faculty adoption is consequential because instructors must decide where the simulation sits in a course, how it is graded, what counts as clinical replacement, and how debriefing/remediation are handled.

## Commercial model

The public terms show a typical higher-education B2B2C structure: institutional/faculty adoption and course configuration combined with student-paid or student-redeemed access codes/enrollment in some implementations. That creates two distinct value propositions:

- **institution:** scalable practice, curricular coverage, faculty insight, clinical-placement relief, outcomes evidence;
- **student:** realistic practice, repeatability, feedback, confidence, and preparation for real patients.

A product strategy that optimizes only one side risks missing the actual adoption system.
