# Shadow Health at a glance — 2026

## One-sentence model

**Shadow Health is Elsevier's nursing-focused patient-encounter and simulation platform: it gives learners repeatable standardized patients to interview, examine, document and reason about, measures the resulting behavior, and is now expanding from screen-based simulation into lab and VR.**

## History

- UF virtual-human communication research begins around 2004.
- Shadow Health founded in Gainesville in 2011 by Benjamin Lok, David Massias and Aaron Kotranza.
- Tina Jones becomes the flagship Digital Standardized Patient.
- Technology matures into a broad nursing DCE platform with an NLP Conversation Engine and SPI performance measurement.
- By 2020 Shadow Health reports about 900 institutions and ~200,000 students served that year.
- Elsevier acquires the company in December 2020; terms are not publicly disclosed.
- In June 2026 Elsevier launches Shadow Health Lab with Virtual Reality, adding 165+ lab simulations and 30 VR encounters.

## Core product

**Student loop:** patient context → conversation → examination → documentation → reasoning → reflection → scoring/feedback → remediation.

**Distinctive assets:**

- own-words patient conversation;
- large standardized patient library;
- clinical-reasoning and communication trace data;
- DCE/SPI measurement and faculty dashboard;
- broad nursing curricular coverage;
- Elsevier ecosystem integration potential;
- new lab + VR modalities.

## Audiences

**Learners:** undergraduate nursing, graduate/advanced-practice nursing, novice/new-graduate nurses.

**Decision makers:** deans/program directors, nursing faculty, simulation leaders, institutional education/purchasing leadership.

## Competitive set

Direct/adjacent competitors include vSim for Nursing (Wolters Kluwer/Laerdal), Kaplan i-Human Patients, Sentinel U, Oxford Medical Simulation, Body Interact, ATI Real Life/Swift River, plus physical simulation labs, standardized patients and clinical placements.

## The strategic question

Shadow Health's historical moat was making a standardized virtual patient **conversational**. That technical capability is becoming easier to reproduce with generative AI. The more defensible future position is likely a validated system that connects realistic interaction to trustworthy measurement and adaptive practice.

The key product challenge is therefore not “How do we build the most human-looking AI patient?” It is:

**How do we create increasingly realistic and variable patient encounters while preserving enough clinical determinism to know what the learner demonstrated, what they missed, and what they should practice next?**
