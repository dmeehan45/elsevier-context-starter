# International nursing-program and simulation variation — 2026

## Purpose

Research bundle for distinguishing durable features of nursing education from U.S.-specific assumptions, especially around program structure, practice hours, simulation, regulation, and the role simulation can legitimately claim in a curriculum.

The examples below are representative, not an exhaustive country-by-country catalogue.

## United Kingdom

### Nursing and Midwifery Council — simulated practice learning

Source: https://www.nmc.org.uk/standards/guidance/supporting-information-for-our-education-and-training-standards/simulated-practice-learning/

Current NMC policy requires 2,300 practice-learning hours for pre-registration nursing programmes and permits up to 600 of those hours to be delivered through simulated practice learning when requirements are met. Simulation can include actors, immersive environments and virtual reality and must be contextualised and supervised as meaningful practice learning.

### Representative curriculum — King's College London Adult Nursing BSc

Sources:
- https://www.kcl.ac.uk/study/undergraduate/courses/nursing-with-registration-as-an-adult-nurse-bsc
- https://www.kcl.ac.uk/study/undergraduate/courses/nursing-with-registration-as-an-adult-nurse-bsc/teaching

King's offers a three-year field-specific Adult Nursing BSc. Roughly half the course is clinical placement. Simulation begins with foundational skills in year one and progresses toward more complex skills, while assessment includes coursework, exams, practical examinations/OSCEs and a practice assessment document completed during placements. King's also describes digital/virtual-placement activity such as documentation, referrals and patient telephone calls.

Unlike the broad U.S. generalist BSN framing, UK pre-registration programmes are commonly organized around registration fields such as adult, children's, mental-health or learning-disability nursing. This can make specialty identity structurally important earlier in the learner journey.

**Product implication:** the UK supports an explicit category of simulation that can carry recognised practice-learning weight, while programme field can materially change patient/case needs.

## Australia

### Australian Nursing and Midwifery Accreditation Council — Registered Nurse Accreditation Standards evidence guide

Source: https://anmac.org.au/sites/default/files/2025-10/anmac_rnas2019_evidence_guide_updated_2025.pdf

The current evidence guide requires entry-to-practice RN programmes to include simulated learning sequenced to prepare students for professional experience placement (PEP). It also requires a minimum of 800 hours of quality PEP, **exclusive of simulation**, across a variety of settings.

### Representative curriculum — University of Sydney Bachelor of Nursing (Advanced Studies)

Sources:
- https://www.sydney.edu.au/courses/courses/uc/bachelor-of-nursing-advanced-studies0.html
- https://www.sydney.edu.au/handbooks/medicine-health/coursework/nursing-advanced-studies/full-time-unit-of-study-table.html

Sydney's current degree is three years full-time and integrates anatomy/physiology, pathophysiology, pharmacology, nursing knowledge/practice, population health, research, clinical/relational skills and specialty contexts. The university reports 920 hours of clinical placement across public/private settings, including mental health, community health and high-acuity experiences, plus dedicated simulation facilities and interprofessional learning.

Sydney also offers graduate-entry/combined pathways that reach nurse registration through a different degree architecture, demonstrating that 'prelicensure nurse' does not imply one entry route even inside a single national system.

**Product implication:** simulation is structurally important, but its clearest regulatory job is preparation, rehearsal and competency development around real clinical placement rather than replacement of the required PEP minimum.

## New Zealand

### Nursing Council of New Zealand — education programme standards

Source: https://nursingcouncil.org.nz/MyNC/NCNZ/News-section/news-item/2024/7/New-enrolled-and-registered-nurse-education-standards.aspx

Current standards require at least 1,000 clinical hours in registered-nurse education and 700 in enrolled-nurse education. The Council states that simulation is excluded from those minimum clinical-hour totals because education providers already use simulation throughout programmes to prepare students for clinical learning.

**Product implication:** as in Australia, the portable value proposition is preparation, standardisation, deliberate practice and evidence of competence—not a broad claim that virtual simulation replaces mandated clinical hours.

## European Union

### Directive 2005/36/EC / consolidated professional-qualification rules

Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02005L0036-20240303

Training for nurses responsible for general care is harmonised around minimum duration requirements: at least three years and 4,600 hours of theoretical and clinical training, with theoretical training at least one-third and clinical training at least one-half of the minimum duration. Member states retain responsibility for organizing education within this framework.

The EU framework therefore creates a stronger common floor for direct clinical training than the state-by-state U.S. model. It does **not** imply identical curricula or identical educational-technology procurement across member states.

## Canada

### Canadian Association of Schools of Nursing — National Nursing Education Framework and simulation guidance

Source: https://www.casn.ca/education/education-home/

CASN provides national educational guidance and treats its Clinical Placements and Simulation Guidelines as a companion to the practice domain of the National Nursing Education Framework. CASN competencies complement, rather than replace, entry-to-practice competencies established by regulatory bodies.

Canada therefore has a national education-quality layer but provincial/territorial regulation remains material. Product research should not treat Canada as one regulatory simulation policy.

### Representative curriculum A — McMaster BScN

Source: https://future.mcmaster.ca/programs/nursing/

McMaster's current BScN uses a problem-based, small-group and self-directed educational model and is offered at both McMaster University and Mohawk College. Students remain at the site to which they are admitted but graduate with a McMaster BScN. This is an international example of **one degree/curriculum delivered through partner sites**, making site implementation and shared governance relevant without necessarily making each site a different product market.

### Representative curriculum B — University of Toronto accelerated BScN

Sources:
- https://bloomberg.nursing.utoronto.ca/learn-with-us/bachelor-of-science-in-nursing/
- https://bloomberg.nursing.utoronto.ca/learn-with-us/bachelor-of-science-in-nursing/what-to-expect-in-the-program/

Toronto's BScN is an accelerated two-year second-entry program with a full schedule of classroom, simulation-lab and clinical learning and no electives. Clinical placements can involve 8- or 12-hour shifts and begin with exposure to older adults and mental-health settings before progressing into acute illness and other practice contexts. In 2026 the program expanded from the St. George campus to the Mississauga campus, making the relationship between a common curriculum and distinct regional clinical ecosystems directly relevant.

### British Columbia — student clinical supervision example

Source: https://www.bccnm.ca/RN/learning/regulatorysupervision/Pages/students.aspx

BCCNM frames student participation in client care through regulatory supervision: nurses decide what students can safely do, set supervision conditions and adjust them as risk changes. This illustrates that real-patient practice is not simply 'exposure'; it carries a professional supervisory relationship that simulation does not reproduce exactly.

## Singapore

### National University of Singapore — Bachelor of Science (Nursing)

Sources:
- https://medicine.nus.edu.sg/nursing/education-programmes/undergraduate/bachelor-of-science-nursing-honours/
- https://medicine.nus.edu.sg/nursing/education-programmes/undergraduate/bachelor-of-science-nursing-honours/curriculum/

NUS describes a three-year BSc (Nursing), with an optional honours year, in which clinical practicum integrates on-campus clinical laboratory learning, simulation experiences and clinical attachments. The progression is explicit: foundation for practice in Year 1, consolidation of theory/practice in Year 2, and transition to novice registered-nurse practice in Year 3.

NUS also notes that students sponsored by hospitals can be posted to their sponsoring hospitals to gain relevant exposure before employment. That connection between education funding, placement and future employer is structurally different from many U.S. programs.

Singapore is useful as a counterexample to overly Western institutional assumptions: a relatively concentrated health system and university landscape can produce tighter coordination among education and clinical partners than the fragmented U.S. clinical-placement market.

## Curriculum dimensions that differ internationally

The examples make several curriculum variables product-relevant:

### Generalist versus earlier field specialization

U.S., Australian, Canadian and Singaporean entry programs commonly prepare broadly for RN practice, while UK learners may enter field-specific registration pathways such as adult nursing. Case libraries and longitudinal learner models need to understand whether specialty is a rotation within a generalist program or the learner's primary registration field.

### Program duration and entry point

Examples range from accelerated two-year second-entry programs to three-year degrees and four-year North American pathways. The same competency can therefore appear at very different calendar points and with learners bringing different prerequisite knowledge.

### Pedagogical architecture

Programs can be course/discipline sequenced, concept-based, problem-based, competency-based or hybrids. McMaster's problem-based model is meaningfully different from a heavily sequenced lecture/lab/clinical model even when graduates seek similar registration outcomes.

### Placement architecture

Placement can be distributed across many independent health systems, concentrated in a national network, tied to sponsoring employers, or delivered through university-health-system partnerships. This changes both exposure variance and who has influence over educational design.

### Simulation's formal status

Simulation can be:

- preparatory to required placement;
- supplementary deliberate practice;
- a recognized portion of regulated practice learning;
- an assessment/OSCE environment;
- a remediation environment.

Those are not interchangeable product claims.

## What appears universal across systems

Across the jurisdictions reviewed, nursing education consistently contains:

- a progression from theoretical/disciplinary knowledge toward increasingly integrated professional practice;
- supervised real-patient clinical learning;
- formal assessment of competence/readiness;
- regulatory and/or professional-body oversight;
- an institutional obligation to ensure appropriate clinical experiences;
- simulation as a safe, controlled and repeatable environment for preparation, integration, rare events and feedback.

## What varies enough to change product strategy

The largest cross-national differences are:

- required practice/clinical hours;
- whether simulation can count toward those hours;
- regulator versus accreditor roles;
- entry pathways, program duration and degree structure;
- generalist versus field-specific registration structure;
- pedagogical model;
- clinical supervision and placement models;
- centralization of the health-care/education system;
- institution-level procurement and technology governance;
- language, cultural and scope-of-practice expectations.

## Cross-market product lesson

'Clinical-hour replacement' is a jurisdiction-specific proposition. **Competency development, standardized exposure, deliberate practice, feedback, remediation and readiness evidence are more portable global product jobs.**

The product should therefore separate the educational value of simulation from any jurisdiction-specific claim about regulatory substitution or practice-hour credit, and should map scenarios to competency progression rather than assume a universal semester/course sequence.
