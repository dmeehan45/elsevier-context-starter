# Nursing student technology access and device environment — 2026

## Purpose

This source bundle supports a practical model of the devices, connectivity, software environments and usage constraints nursing learners encounter. It distinguishes:

1. what students personally own or commonly use;
2. what nursing programs require or provision;
3. what is permitted in classrooms, simulation and clinical settings;
4. what current Elsevier/Shadow Health products technically require.

## Higher-education baseline

### EDUCAUSE — primary and secondary academic devices

Sources:
- https://er.educause.edu/articles/2021/11/educause-quickpoll-results-flexibility-and-equity-for-student-success
- https://er.educause.edu/articles/2023/1/the-evolving-landscape-of-students-mobile-learning-practices-in-higher-education
- https://www.educause.edu/ecar/research-publications/student-technology-report-supporting-the-whole-student/2020/technology-use-and-environmental-preferences

Durable observations:

- Laptop and smartphone form the dominant higher-education device pair.
- In the 2021 EDUCAUSE QuickPoll, 81% of respondents used a laptop as their primary academic device and 56% used a smartphone as their secondary device.
- EDUCAUSE's 2020 work found three-quarters of students connecting to campus Wi-Fi used both a smartphone and laptop.
- Students use secondary devices for mobility, quick tasks, cameras/scanning, backup when a primary device or Wi-Fi fails, and multiple-screen workflows.
- Wi-Fi reliability and power access are infrastructure dependencies, not conveniences.

These figures are broader higher-education evidence, not nursing-specific prevalence estimates.

### Access is not the same as adequacy

Sources:
- https://www.educause.edu/ecar/research-publications/2021/student-experiences-with-connectivity-and-technology-in-the-pandemic/some-devices-arent-up-to-the-task
- https://er.educause.edu/articles/2021/11/educause-quickpoll-results-flexibility-and-equity-for-student-success
- https://www.educause.edu/ecar/research-publications/2022/students-and-technology-report-rebalancing-the-student-experience/technology-challenges-and-solutions

EDUCAUSE has repeatedly found that near-universal device access does not eliminate technology friction. Device malfunctions, underpowered hardware, incompatible software and unstable Internet connections can disrupt classes, exams and coursework and create significant stress.

This matters for nursing because programs commonly depend on secure testing software, video/web conferencing, LMS platforms, browser simulations and specialty applications that impose stricter requirements than basic web browsing.

## Nursing-specific device ownership/use evidence

### Undergraduate nursing mobile-device survey

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33259348/

In one quantitative undergraduate nursing survey, 87.6% of participants owned smartphones, 76% laptops and 47.1% tablets. Learners used mobile devices for academic tasks, communication/collaboration and electronic-resource access. First-year learners had lower laptop/tablet ownership and usage than learners later in the program.

This study should not be treated as a current U.S. national prevalence estimate; its value is showing the multi-device pattern within nursing and that device access can vary by learner stage.

### Nursing-student digital technology in clinical education

Sources:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38997198/
- https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S8755722324000681
- https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1557308726001447

Recent systematic/scoping reviews find that nursing students perceive digital tools as potentially useful for access to knowledge, communication, competency development and clinical decision support, while also reporting barriers related to institutional policy, usability, professionalism, privacy, distraction and inconsistent implementation.

The 2026 scoping review of smartphone use in clinical practice concludes that smartphones have pedagogical potential but require clear institutional policies and ethical/equitable implementation.

## Current nursing-school requirements — representative U.S. examples

These examples are useful for understanding the practical hardware floor imposed by nursing education. They are not intended as a statistically representative sample of all schools.

### Johns Hopkins School of Nursing

Source: https://nursing.jhu.edu/student-experience/accepted-students/computer/

- students are required to own a reliable laptop;
- Windows or Mac is supported;
- current guidance recommends 16 GB RAM and current full desktop operating systems;
- Chromebooks and many tablets, including iPads and Android tablets, do not meet minimum program requirements;
- webcam/headphones may be necessary for online instruction and proctored exams;
- high-speed home Internet is required;
- hospital/employer networks may block video/web applications through restrictive firewalls.

### Duke University School of Nursing

Source: https://nursing.duke.edu/student-resources/technical-support-tech-hub/student-technology-resources

All nursing students are required to have their own fully functioning laptop running a currently supported Apple or Microsoft operating system.

### University of Michigan School of Nursing

Source: https://teamdynamix.umich.edu/TDClient/57/Portal/KB/PrintArticle?ID=10273

All undergraduate and graduate nursing students must have access to a laptop. Recommended peripherals include webcams/headsets for synchronous online work and adapters for classroom presentation.

### Ohio State College of Nursing

Source: https://nursing.osu.edu/technology-services

Current recommendations support Windows and Mac computers, recommend 16 GB RAM, current browsers, Office 365 and Zoom, and specify broadband expectations.

### Chamberlain University

Source: https://www.chamberlain.edu/academic-resources/technology-requirements

- all students must own a laptop/computer and have high-speed Internet;
- Windows and macOS are supported;
- 8 GB RAM minimum / 16 GB recommended;
- webcam and microphone are expected;
- Chromebooks are not supported;
- mobile devices can access some course content but cannot provide all functionality;
- iPads cannot be used for some secure testing workflows such as Examplify.

This is particularly relevant because Chamberlain serves a large, multi-campus and online nursing population.

### Rutgers School of Nursing / Rutgers-Camden

Sources:
- https://nursing.rutgers.edu/technology/bring-your-own-device/
- https://nursing.camden.rutgers.edu/laptop-requirements

Rutgers operates a formal BYOD model. Nursing learners bring personally owned devices to class and take course examinations on them. Current compatibility rules support Windows/macOS laptops while excluding or limiting Chromebooks, iPads/mobile devices, Linux and unsupported processor/OS combinations for testing.

### University of Maryland School of Nursing

Source: https://www.nursing.umaryland.edu/technology/students/

Current 2026 guidance requires students outside fully online programs to have a laptop and explicitly notes that iPads are not supported for exams.

### Texas A&M College of Nursing

Source: https://nursing.tamu.edu/students/current/technology-requirements.html

Traditional and second-degree BSN learners must have a laptop for academic and testing purposes; online RN-to-BSN, certificate and graduate learners may use a desktop or laptop. Mobile devices are not supported for exams.

### Duquesne University School of Nursing

Source: https://www.duq.edu/academics/colleges-and-schools/nursing/technology-requirements.php

Current 2026-27 requirements make the laptop mandatory, describe an iPad as optional for ebooks and point-of-care apps, and highly recommend a current smartphone. The iPad is framed as a companion rather than laptop replacement.

### Binghamton Decker School of Nursing

Sources:
- https://www.binghamton.edu/decker/nursing/undergraduate/requirements.html
- https://www.binghamton.edu/decker/nursing/graduate/requirements.html

Students may use a laptop or iPad to write papers, create presentations, record video and take exams. This is an important counterexample to the more common laptop-only testing pattern and demonstrates institution-level variation.

### University of Rochester School of Nursing

Source: https://son.rochester.edu/current-students/courses/technical-requirements.html

The ABSN and RN-to-BS iROC initiative provisions students with iPads configured with course materials and security controls. This illustrates an institution-provisioned tablet model rather than universal learner-owned device assumptions.

## Smartphone use in clinical environments

### Required or encouraged point-of-care use

NYU Rory Meyers College of Nursing requires learners entering clinical sequences to have a smartphone/PDA for email and point-of-care drug/clinical-reference software.

Source: https://nursing.nyu.edu/current-students/clinical-affairs/requirements

Duquesne similarly frames iPads as optional devices for ebooks and point-of-care apps and recommends a current smartphone.

### Restricted use and professionalism/privacy controls

Representative current policies:
- Penn Nursing: https://www.nursing.upenn.edu/students/handbooks-forms/bsn-handbook/clinical-information-policies/
- Penn State 2026 handbook: https://www.nursing.psu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Second-Degree-BSN-Student-Handbook-2.17.26.pdf
- South Dakota State: https://www.sdstate.edu/nursing/nursing-student-handbook
- Boise State: https://www.boisestate.edu/nursing/student-resources/son-student-policy-manual/section-2-son-school-wide-policies/electronic-devices-and-social-media-policy-son-04/

Across these examples, personal electronic devices in clinical settings are subject to facility and instructor policy. Typical constraints include:

- clinically related use only;
- no personal communication/social media;
- no photography/recording;
- no storage of identifiable patient information;
- devices silent or restricted;
- hospital-provided devices/workstations may supersede personal-device use.

Therefore smartphone familiarity does **not** imply that a nursing product can assume uninterrupted personal-phone use during clinical practice.

## Institution-provided and specialty hardware

A national study of telehealth in nursing education found that schools used institution-provided iPads/tablets/computers, telehealth carts/kits, cellular phones and a smaller number of telepresence robots/peripherals.

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9371618/

AR/VR and specialized simulation hardware should generally be modeled as institutional infrastructure rather than assumed learner-owned equipment.

## Current Elsevier / Shadow Health technical environment

### Shadow Health

Sources:
- https://service.elsevier.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/34713/supporthub/shadow-health/p/17209/
- https://service.elsevier.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/34695/supporthub/shadow-health/
- https://service.elsevier.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/34883/supporthub/shadow-health/p/17209/

As of 2026:

- Shadow Health is browser-delivered SaaS using WebGL/JavaScript;
- supported use is desktop/laptop rather than a native mobile app;
- continuous Internet access is required;
- minimum network speed is 3 Mbps download;
- current browsers include Chrome, Firefox, Edge and Safari;
- headphones are recommended for audio fidelity, including heart sounds;
- speech-to-text can use the learner's microphone in supported browsers;
- Elsevier states that Chrome is the most popular browser among Shadow Health learners.

Elsevier's broader nursing/health eProduct system-requirements page currently lists Shadow Health for desktop/laptop on Windows/macOS, not Chromebook/tablet/phone.

Source: https://service.elsevier.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/8199/supporthub/evolve/role/student/

### Shadow Health already captures hardware telemetry

The current IT/legal FAQ states that Shadow Health collects non-personally-identifiable client information including:

- browser;
- operating system and version;
- video-card manufacturer/version;
- CPU manufacturer/version/frequency;
- main memory;
- video memory and shader model.

Elsevier says this information is used to establish development hardware targets and for customer support.

This is strategically important: the exact device mix of actual Shadow Health users may be answerable with internal aggregate telemetry rather than external proxies. Public documentation does not reveal the current Mac/Windows share, hardware-performance distribution or variation by learner/program/account.

## Durable observations

1. **Laptop + smartphone is the dominant learner device ecology.** The laptop carries high-friction academic work; the smartphone carries communication, quick lookup, cameras/scanning and point-of-care access.
2. **A full laptop remains a practical baseline in nursing education.** Secure testing and specialty applications frequently exclude Chromebooks, mobile OS devices and underpowered hardware.
3. **Tablet usage is heterogeneous.** At some institutions tablets are optional companions; at others iPads are provisioned or accepted as primary devices.
4. **Clinical settings are a separate technology environment.** Personal smartphones may be valuable, restricted or prohibited depending on patient privacy, professionalism and facility policy.
5. **Connectivity matters.** Nursing learners may study from home, campus, work or clinical environments, and hospital/corporate firewalls can block educational applications even when bandwidth is adequate.
6. **Device ownership should not be treated as device adequacy.** Compatibility, processing power, operating-system support, browser/GPU support and reliable Internet all affect access.
7. **Shadow Health's current architecture is aligned primarily to laptop/desktop workflows**, even though its learners are highly familiar with smartphones and may use tablets elsewhere in the learning journey.
8. **Actual Shadow Health client telemetry is likely the strongest source for hardware-target decisions** if internal teams can aggregate it appropriately.
