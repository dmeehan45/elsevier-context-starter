# Nursing and Shadow Health acronym reference sources — 2026

## Purpose

Source bundle for a role-specific acronym catalogue for product work on Shadow Health. The scope is intentionally broader than nursing licensure terminology but narrower than a medical-abbreviation dictionary. It covers the language most likely to appear in conversations about nursing education, simulation, assessment, accreditation, graduate nursing, clinical judgment, educational technology, privacy/accessibility, and the Shadow Health/HESI ecosystem.

## Shadow Health and Elsevier

### Shadow Health product terminology

- https://www.elsevier.com/products/shadow-health
- https://service.elsevier.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/34854/supporthub/shadow-health/
- https://service.elsevier.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/34719/supporthub/shadow-health/
- https://www.elsevier.com/resources/demonstrating-efficacy-in-clinical-judgment-skills-using-the-digital-clinical-experience

Current public materials establish the terms Digital Clinical Experience (DCE), Digital Standardized Patient (DSP), Student Performance Index (SPI), electronic health record (EHR), and the relationship between Shadow Health experiences and clinical-judgment constructs. Public support documentation is particularly important because the DCE score and SPI are not interchangeable: the DCE score is the percentage Elsevier recommends for most grading workflows, while SPI/proficiency reporting is intended to represent performance on the underlying activities/clinical-reasoning dimensions.

### HESI assessment terminology

- https://www.elsevier.com/products/hesi/hesi-exam-solutions-nursing
- https://www.elsevier.com/en-gb/resources/2022-scientific-evidence-for-the-hesi-admission-assessment-a2-for-nursing-and-healthcare-education-programs
- https://www.elsevier.com/resources/hesi-pn-validity-study

Current HESI materials use Admission Assessment (A2), Exit Exam (often E2 in the market), Computer Adaptive Test (CAT), Clinical Practice Readiness Assessment (CPRA), specialty exams and remediation. Current Elsevier pages generally treat HESI as a brand rather than spelling it out; avoid forcing the historical company-name expansion into normal product usage.

## Licensure, regulation and clinical judgment

### NCSBN, NCLEX, NGN and NCJMM

- https://www.ncsbn.org/news/ncsbn-launches-next-generation-nclex-exam
- https://www.ncsbn.org/public-files/L2L_Fall2019.pdf
- https://www.ncsbn.org/policy
- https://www.nclex.com/clinical-judgment-measurement-model.page

NCSBN launched the Next Generation NCLEX (NGN) in 2023. Its official term is the **NCSBN Clinical Judgment Measurement Model (NCJMM)**. NCSBN materials have also used the shorter CJMM in some historical publications; current NCSBN/NCLEX materials prefer NCJMM. The model includes six commonly referenced cognitive operations: recognize cues, analyze cues, prioritize hypotheses, generate solutions, take actions, and evaluate outcomes.

NCSBN also uses computerized adaptive testing (CAT) for NCLEX administration and is the central national organization through which state boards of nursing collaborate. The Nurse Licensure Compact (NLC) is a separate regulatory construct from national certification.

## Academic nursing, accreditation and credentials

### AACN and CCNE

- https://www.aacnnursing.org/
- https://www.aacnnursing.org/essentials
- https://www.aacnnursing.org/ccne-accreditation/accreditation-resources/standards-procedures-guidelines

AACN here means the **American Association of Colleges of Nursing**, the national voice for academic nursing. This is a high-risk acronym collision because **AACN can also mean the American Association of Critical-Care Nurses** in clinical contexts. CCNE is the Commission on Collegiate Nursing Education, AACN's autonomous accrediting arm for baccalaureate and graduate nursing education and related programs.

### ACEN

- https://www.acenursing.org/

ACEN is the Accreditation Commission for Education in Nursing and accredits nursing education across multiple program levels. In product conversations, ACEN, CCNE and NLN CNEA should not be treated as synonyms even though all are nursing accreditors.

### NLN and NLN CNEA

- https://www.nln.org/
- https://cnea.nln.org/
- https://www.nln.org/certification/Certification-for-Nurse-Educators/cne
- https://www.nln.org/certification/Certification-for-Nurse-Educators/cne-cl

NLN is the National League for Nursing. Its autonomous accreditation division is the NLN Commission for Nursing Education Accreditation (NLN CNEA). NLN also awards educator certifications such as Certified Nurse Educator (CNE) and Certified Academic Clinical Nurse Educator (CNEcl).

### ANA / ANCC nursing roles and credentials

- https://www.nursingworld.org/practice-policy/scope-of-practice/
- https://www.nursingworld.org/certification/how-to-display-your-credentials/

ANA materials distinguish educational degrees, licensure, state advanced-practice designations and national certification. APRN roles include nurse practitioner, clinical nurse specialist, certified nurse-midwife and certified registered nurse anesthetist/anesthesiologist. Credential strings therefore often combine degree + license + role/state designation + board certification.

## Simulation and nursing-education standards

### INACSL

- https://www.inacsl.org/healthcare-simulation-standards

INACSL is the International Nursing Association for Clinical Simulation and Learning. Its Healthcare Simulation Standards of Best Practice (HSSOBP) cover prebriefing, design, facilitation, debriefing, operations, objectives/outcomes, professional integrity, simulation-enhanced interprofessional education, evaluation, and professional development. INACSL uses simulation-based education (SBE) as a general term.

### SSH

- https://www.ssih.org/

SSH is the Society for Simulation in Healthcare. Common credentials include Certified Healthcare Simulation Educator (CHSE) and Certified Healthcare Simulation Operations Specialist (CHSOS), with advanced variants CHSE-A and CHSOS-A.

### QSEN

- https://www.qsen.org/competencies-pre-licensure-ksas

QSEN is Quality and Safety Education for Nurses. Its six competency areas are patient-centered care, teamwork/collaboration, evidence-based practice, quality improvement, safety and informatics. QSEN describes associated KSAs: knowledge, skills and attitudes.

### Clinical communication

- https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/communication/tools/sbar.html

AHRQ's TeamSTEPPS materials define SBAR as Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation/Request, one of the most common structured communication frameworks in nursing education and practice.

## Assessment and competitor vocabulary

### ATI Nursing Education

- https://www.atitesting.com/educator/solutions/comprehensive-predictor
- https://www.atitesting.com/educator/about/news/2025/09/04/teas-scores-are-strong-predictor-of-nursing-student-success
- https://www.atitesting.com/educator/solutions/ati-pulse/faq

ATI uses terms including Test of Essential Academic Skills (TEAS), Content Mastery Series (CMS), Comprehensive Predictor (CP), and Probability of Passing (PoP). These are market/product terms rather than universal nursing-education standards, so catalogue entries mark them as competitor vocabulary.

## Education technology and interoperability

### 1EdTech

- https://www.1edtech.org/
- https://standards.1edtech.org/

Relevant standards include Learning Tools Interoperability (LTI), Question & Test Interoperability (QTI), Competencies and Academic Standards Exchange (CASE), Caliper Analytics, Comprehensive Learner Record (CLR) and EduAPI. Current Elsevier guidance supports LTI 1.3 for Shadow Health workflows with compatible LMSs.

### Healthcare interoperability

- https://hl7.org/fhir/

FHIR means Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources and is published by HL7. These terms are not core nursing pedagogy, but they become relevant when discussing EHR/simulation data, interoperability or future clinical-system integration.

## Student data, health data, accessibility and security

### FERPA

- https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/faq/what-ferpa
- https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/privacy-and-data-sharing

FERPA is the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act. In postsecondary education, learner records and identifiable educational-performance data are generally an education-data question before they are a HIPAA question.

### HIPAA / FERPA boundary

- https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/ferpa-hipaa/index.html
- https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/does-ferpa-or-hipaa-apply-to-records-on-students-at-health-clinics/index.html

HIPAA is the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act. HHS guidance clarifies that records covered by FERPA are excluded from the HIPAA Privacy Rule definition of PHI. Product teams should therefore avoid treating all health-related educational data as automatically HIPAA-covered.

### WCAG

- https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/

WCAG is the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines, a W3C standard. Accessibility review in higher education commonly also surfaces ADA and VPAT terminology.

## International terms likely to appear in global product conversations

Existing research in `source-nursing-program-variation-global-2026` supports the following recurring abbreviations:

- **NMC** — Nursing and Midwifery Council (United Kingdom)
- **ANMAC** — Australian Nursing and Midwifery Accreditation Council
- **AHPRA** — Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency
- **NCNZ** — Nursing Council of New Zealand
- **CASN** — Canadian Association of Schools of Nursing
- **BCCNM** — British Columbia College of Nurses and Midwives

These should remain recognition-level vocabulary unless the role moves into a specific international implementation.

## Interpretation rules for the living catalogue

1. Prefer the current official organization/product name over historical acronym expansions.
2. Flag collisions instead of pretending an acronym has one universal meaning.
3. Distinguish universal/professional terminology from vendor vocabulary.
4. Do not expand the catalogue into a general medical-abbreviation dictionary; disease, medication, lab and order abbreviations belong in a separate clinical-content reference if needed.
5. When an internal Elsevier term conflicts with a public/domain meaning, preserve both and label the internal usage explicitly after it is verified.
