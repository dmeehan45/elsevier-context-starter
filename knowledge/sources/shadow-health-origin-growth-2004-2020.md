# Shadow Health origin and growth, 2004–2020

## What this is

A grouped public-source record for the pre-acquisition history of Shadow Health and the University of Florida research from which it emerged.

## Durable observations

- The underlying virtual-human research began at the University of Florida around 2004 and centered on whether interaction with virtual characters could improve real-world communication skills.
- Shadow Health was co-founded in Gainesville in 2011 by Benjamin Lok, David Massias, and Aaron Kotranza after UF licensed the relevant university technology.
- By August 2012, the company had raised more than $1 million across two financing rounds, hired 19 employees, beta-tested three prototypes, and launched its signature virtual-patient product centered on Tina Jones.
- UF described the technology as natural-language-processing-based and reported that the digital patient technology was patented in 2018.
- Shadow Health grew from two people at commercial launch to roughly 150 employees by the time of the Elsevier acquisition. The company reported serving three nursing schools in its first year versus roughly 900 in 2020, and close to 200,000 students in 2020.
- The original problem framing emphasized communication, empathy, patient interaction, and the academic-to-practice gap, not test preparation alone.

## Caveats

Growth figures are company/UF-reported and should be treated as historical point-in-time figures rather than current installed-base counts.
