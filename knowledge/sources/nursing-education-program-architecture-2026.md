## Representative entry-level curricula

### University of Washington BSN

- Program: https://nursing.uw.edu/academics/bsn/
- 2026 curriculum: https://nursing.uw.edu/academics/bsn/2026-curriculum/

The UW BSN is a useful representative model of a two-year upper-division nursing curriculum entered after college-level prerequisites. The sequence moves from health assessment, foundational skills, pharmacology/pathophysiology, professional nursing and community health into adult illness care, then psychiatric, pediatric and childbearing-family nursing, and finally a transition-to-clinical-practice practicum. UW reports more than 600 hours of hands-on patient care and explicitly pairs simulation with supervised clinical placements.

### UW Accelerated BSN

- Program: https://nursing.uw.edu/academics/accelerated-bsn/
- 2026 curriculum: https://nursing.uw.edu/academics/accelerated-bsn/2026-curriculum/

The ABSN compresses the same broad entry-to-practice progression into four consecutive quarters for learners who already hold a non-nursing bachelor's degree and have completed prerequisites.

### Community College of Baltimore County ADN

- Program and curriculum map: https://www.ccbcmd.edu/Programs-and-Courses-Finder/Program/associate-degree-nursing---registered-nursing-program.html

The associate-degree pathway combines general education and sciences with a sequenced nursing core including professional role, fundamentals, health assessment, adult nursing concepts, maternal-newborn, mental health and pediatrics. The program describes theoretical, clinical and high-fidelity laboratory study.

### Johns Hopkins MSN Entry into Nursing

- Program and current curriculum: https://nursing.jhu.edu/programs/masters-entry/

This is a four-semester prelicensure graduate-entry program for learners with a bachelor's degree in another field. The current curriculum illustrates the same generalist progression at a faster graduate-entry pace: professional/foundational practice, mental health and communication, pharmacology/pathophysiology, chronic adult care and public health, complex adult care plus pediatrics/perinatal, then transition to practice, licensure preparation, leadership and synthesis clinical.

## Representative advanced-practice curricula

### Duke MSN Family Nurse Practitioner

- Curriculum: https://nursing.duke.edu/academic-programs/curricula/msn-family

The specialty sequence includes advanced-practice primary care didactic courses, staged clinical courses with documented clinical hours, and synthesis. It illustrates how advanced-practice programs move beyond generalist RN preparation toward diagnosis, management and role-specific practice.

### Johns Hopkins DNP Advanced Practice simulation

- Simulation programs: https://nursing.jhu.edu/faculty-research/research/areas-of-expertise/policy-leadership-administration/the-center-for-simulation-and-immersive-learning/programs/

Johns Hopkins describes a full-scale DNP advanced-practice simulation curriculum using high-fidelity manikins, procedural task trainers, virtual simulation, VR, standardized patients and OSCEs, including onsite immersions for distance learners.

### AACN advanced-level scenarios

- Scenario library: https://www.aacnnursing.org/essentials/tool-kit/nursing-practice-scenario
- Example advanced telehealth scenario: https://www.aacnnursing.org/essentials/tool-kit/nursing-practice-scenario/clinical-assessment-and-management-of-depression-in-a-primary-care-telehealth-setting

AACN's advanced-level scenarios emphasize integrated assessment, diagnostic reasoning, management, professional communication and evidence-based decision making. This differs materially from early prelicensure simulation focused on foundational nursing performance and safe clinical judgment.

## Durable observations

1. Nursing education is increasingly organized around longitudinal competency development rather than only course completion. AACN distinguishes Level 1 entry-level and Level 2 advanced-level sub-competencies and progression indicators.
2. Programs still operationalize that competency model through courses, semesters/quarters, prerequisites, clinical sequences, grades, progression rules and accreditation requirements.
3. Clinical practice is not one thing. Programs combine classroom/didactic learning, skills/lab practice, simulation, supervised real-patient clinical rotations, precepted practica, reflection/debriefing, and assessment.
4. Simulation is a methodology inside the curriculum, not an endpoint. Its strongest value is providing standardized, repeatable, observable practice for competencies or patient situations that are unsafe, scarce, variable or difficult to guarantee in real clinical placements.
5. Prelicensure programs use simulation alongside broad generalist preparation across the lifespan. Advanced-practice programs use it more heavily for advanced assessment, diagnosis/management, procedures, communication, telehealth, interprofessional practice and structured competency assessment such as OSCEs.
6. The staff workflow is as important as the learner workflow. Faculty must map activities to competencies, prepare learners, facilitate/debrief, evaluate performance and remediate; simulation teams schedule space/technology and run operations; clinical-placement teams contract with sites and manage compliance; program leaders own outcomes and accreditation evidence.
