## Durable observations

NCSBN's landmark national simulation study found that **high-quality** simulation could substitute for up to 50% of traditional clinical hours in prelicensure nursing curricula under appropriate program conditions. That result continues to anchor U.S. simulation policy discussions, but it is not a blanket nationwide authorization: state boards, accreditors, institutions, program design, faculty capability, and specific modality all matter.

INACSL's 2025 standards reinforce that effective simulation requires much more than scenario software. Standards cover simulation design, prebriefing, facilitation, debriefing, operations, objectives/outcomes, professional integrity, interprofessional education, evaluation, and professional development.

## Strategic implication

A simulation vendor's implementation materials, facilitation/debriefing support, analytics, and evidence base are part of the product value—not merely ancillary services—because programs must demonstrate quality and educational rigor when simulation is integrated into curriculum or substituted for clinical experience.

The implication is synthesis from the standards.
