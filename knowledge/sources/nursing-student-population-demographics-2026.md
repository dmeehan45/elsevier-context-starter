# Nursing student population demographics and pathway mix — 2026

## Purpose

This source bundle describes who is enrolled in U.S. nursing education and, more importantly, how learner age and life stage vary systematically by educational pathway. It should not be read as a single national portrait of “the nursing student.”

## Current scale of academic nursing

### AACN 2025-2026 annual survey

Sources:
- https://www.aacnnursing.org/news-data/all-news/article/schools-of-nursing-see-enrollment-increases-across-most-program-levels-signaling-strong-interest-in-nursing-careers-1480
- https://www.aacnnursing.org/news-data/all-news/rounds-with-leadership-a-deeper-dive-into-the-data

AACN surveyed 998 U.S. schools offering baccalaureate and graduate nursing education in fall 2025. Entry-level baccalaureate enrollment reached 283,303 students, increasing 7.6% in one year. Enrollment also increased in master's and DNP programs while PhD enrollment declined.

AACN reported 821,491 applications to baccalaureate and higher-degree nursing programs in 2025. More than 93,000 qualified applications were not offered admission, overwhelmingly at the entry-level baccalaureate level. Application counts are not unique people, because one applicant can apply to multiple programs.

Current graduate enrollment is strongly role-oriented. In 2025:
- nurse practitioner majors represented 66.3% of master's enrollment;
- NP majors represented 68.5% of post-baccalaureate DNP enrollment;
- post-master's DNP enrollment was led by Leadership at 39.3%, followed by Generalist at 21.4%, NP at 16.5%, and Advanced Practice at 10.1%.

This means “graduate nursing student” is not one population. The dominant post-BSN graduate learner is often pursuing a new advanced-practice role, while the post-MSN DNP population contains a large concentration of established clinicians moving toward leadership, systems, or terminal practice preparation.

## Age differs sharply by pathway

### National League for Nursing annual survey, 2023

Source: https://www.nln.org/docs/default-source/research-statistics/2023-nln-annual-survey-results/proportion-of-student-enrollment-by-age-and-program-type-2023.pdf

The NLN survey provides a particularly useful age distribution by program type:

| Program | Under 25 | 26-30 | 31-40 | 41-50 | 51-60 | 61+ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Generic BSN | 77.4% | 11.1% | 7.7% | 2.5% | 0.7% | 0.6% |
| ADN | 38.5% | 25.0% | 24.8% | 9.3% | 2.2% | 0.3% |
| PN/VN | 39.9% | 25.5% | 24.4% | 8.5% | 1.6% | 0.3% |
| RN-to-BSN | 17.4% | 23.0% | 30.0% | 17.2% | 6.4% | 5.2% |
| MSN | 26.0% | 29.0% | 26.5% | 13.0% | 3.8% | 1.2% |
| Doctorate | 5.1% | 16.2% | 37.3% | 25.8% | 13.1% | 2.5% |

The NLN invited 980 member schools and received 364 responses, so these are survey estimates rather than a census of every U.S. nursing student.

### Durable interpretation

Age is not noise around one nursing-student persona. It is structurally associated with the educational pathway.

- The generic BSN is predominantly a first-degree, early-adult pathway.
- ADN and PN/VN programs contain much larger concentrations of learners in their late 20s and 30s, including career changers and learners combining education with adult responsibilities.
- RN-to-BSN education is primarily post-licensure adult education.
- Doctoral nursing education is overwhelmingly an established-career population: 78.6% of doctoral students in the NLN survey were over age 30.

For product research, pathway should therefore be treated as a proxy for prior clinical experience, career identity, time constraints and expected instructional treatment—not just credential level.

## Gender and racial/ethnic representation

### NLN prelicensure RN programs

Sources:
- https://www.nln.org/docs/default-source/research-statistics/2023-nln-annual-survey-results/2023-nln-annual-survey-of-schools-of-nursing-executive-summary.pdf
- https://www.nln.org/detail-pages/news/2024/09/23/nursing-schools-programs-make-progress-in-diversifying-nursing-students-workforce-according-to-new-nln-survey

For 2023, NLN reported that underrepresented students made up 48.7% of prelicensure RN enrollment. Men represented 14.2% of basic RN students; transgender, genderqueer, or nonbinary learners were reported at 0.1% in the survey.

The NLN also reported growth in Hispanic enrollment to 13.8% and continued movement in the racial/ethnic composition of prelicensure education. The precise categories differ across datasets, so comparisons across AACN, NLN, Census, and workforce surveys should not be treated as perfectly interchangeable.

### AACN baccalaureate and graduate programs

Source: https://www.aacnnursing.org/news-data/all-news/rounds-with-leadership-a-deeper-dive-into-the-data

AACN reported increasing male representation across program types in 2025. Male students represented approximately:
- 12.6% of baccalaureate students;
- 11.8% of master's students;
- 16.0% of DNP students;
- 11.5% of PhD students.

The gender concentration remains substantial: nursing education is still predominantly female, although the exact mix varies by pathway and has been gradually diversifying.

## Accelerated / second-degree learners

Source: https://www.aacnnursing.org/news-data/fact-sheets/accelerated-baccalaureate-and-masters-degrees-in-nursing

AACN reported 29,421 students enrolled in accelerated baccalaureate programs and 11,249 in entry-level master's programs in 2025. Accelerated pathways serve people who already hold a non-nursing bachelor's degree and compress entry-to-practice preparation into an intensive full-time experience.

AACN characterizes the typical second-degree learner as older, motivated, academically experienced and eager for clinical learning. Programs are sufficiently intensive that AACN advises prospective learners that working while enrolled is generally discouraged.

This creates a distinct learner segment: someone may be a clinical novice while simultaneously being an experienced adult, prior college graduate and established professional.

## Degree-completion learners

Source: https://www.aacnnursing.org/news-data/fact-sheets/degree-completion-programs-for-rns

As of May 2026, AACN reported 701 RN-to-BSN programs nationally, more than 600 at least partially online. Typical completion time is 1-2 years. Enrollment remains below its 2018 peak, despite modest recent increases.

RN-to-BSN learners should be treated as licensed clinicians returning for credential completion rather than as novices progressing through initial professional socialization. They commonly bring employment, previous nursing education, patient-care experience and an existing professional identity into the classroom.

## What the aggregate data does not tell us well

National academic datasets are strong on enrollment, degree level, age, sex/gender and broad racial/ethnic categories. They are much weaker on:

- marital/partner status;
- dependent children or elder-care responsibilities;
- weekly paid work hours;
- household income and financial precarity;
- first-generation-college status across all program types;
- prior occupations among career changers;
- commute/travel burden for clinical and simulation;
- immigration status and international-student experience;
- disability and accommodation needs;
- reasons for choosing a specific nursing pathway;
- specialty aspirations and how they change over time.

Those variables require cohort studies, student surveys, institutional data and qualitative research rather than relying only on national enrollment datasets.
