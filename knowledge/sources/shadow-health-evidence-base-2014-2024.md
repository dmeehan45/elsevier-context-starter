# Shadow Health evidence base — selected research, 2014–2024

## What this is

A selected public research record for evidence about Shadow Health and virtual-patient simulation. This is not a systematic review of every Shadow Health publication.

## Sources

- Kleinheksel, **“Transformative Learning through Virtual Patient Simulations”**, Clinical Simulation in Nursing (2014): https://www.nursingsimulation.org/article/S1876-1399%2814%2900030-9/fulltext
- Jimenez, Kleinheksel, Kotranza, **Student Performance Index technical report** (2015): DOI 10.13140/RG.2.1.5068.5283
- National Academies workshop discussion of Shadow Health and other simulation technologies (2018): https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK532781/
- Wang et al., **“Developing Nursing Students' Practice Readiness with Shadow Health Digital Clinical Experiences”** (2023 conference chapter): https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10455979
- Albagawi et al., **“The Lived Experiences of Saudi Nursing Students in Digital Clinical Experience”** (2024): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10924629/
- Williams et al., virtual-patient simulation pilot in prelicensure nursing (2024): https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1876139924000811

## What the evidence supports

Across the selected literature, virtual-patient simulation is associated with benefits in safe/repeatable practice, confidence, critical thinking, clinical reasoning, reflection, and bridging theory to practice. The 2024 phenomenological Shadow Health study found themes of comfort/safety, critical thinking/problem solving, appraisal of knowledge, and transition to practice.

## Evidence limitations

- Much of the broad evidence base is about virtual simulation generally rather than Shadow Health specifically.
- Several Shadow Health-specific studies are small, single-institution, qualitative, quasi-experimental, or authored/co-authored by people affiliated with Shadow Health/Elsevier.
- Learner perceptions and within-simulation scores are useful but are not equivalent to demonstrated real-world patient outcomes or long-term practice readiness.
- Comparative evidence against contemporary AI-native or VR-native competitors is limited.

## Extracted knowledge

Supports treating simulation efficacy as credible while keeping stronger causal claims about workplace readiness appropriately provisional.
