## Durable observations

Shadow Health maintains two distinct score concepts:

- **DCE Score**: a normalized percentage Elsevier recommends for grading. In clinical-replacement contexts it may also be used for threshold/pass-fail completion.
- **Student Performance Index (SPI)**: described by Elsevier as a valid and reliable measure of clinical judgment/critical-thinking performance on patient-exam assignments. It can include subjective data collection, objective data collection, education/empathy, care plans, information processing, patient teaching, prescription writing, and medication selection depending on assignment.

Elsevier explicitly says the raw SPI is **not** recommended as a direct grading percentage because it is not normalized like the DCE score. SPI proficiency bands compare learners with representative student populations.

The faculty-only Practice Readiness Dashboard exposes course summary, student performance, engagement, assignment summary, and category-level strengths/weaknesses views.

## Strategic significance

The important asset is not only the simulated patient. Shadow Health also observes a learner's sequence of questions/actions and turns that trace into structured feedback and cohort-level analytics. That data exhaust is potentially more strategically defensible than visual fidelity alone if it can be linked to external outcomes.

The final sentence is synthesis and should not be treated as a proven moat.
