## Operating model visible in documentation

The documentation presents Shadow Health as configurable courseware rather than one fixed learning experience. Faculty can sequence assignments, set open and due dates, change descriptions and prerequisites, hide or expose activities, and configure the amount of guidance, feedback, remediation, and repeated practice available to students.

Three pedagogical patterns are explicit:

- **Mastery Learning**: formative use with immediate feedback and remediation.
- **Clinical Reasoning Check**: more summative use intended to differentiate learner performance and surface deficiencies.
- **Hybrid/custom**: formative and higher-stakes configurations can coexist across a course.

The actual student experience can therefore differ materially by instructor configuration and by product integration such as Sherpath Vantage.

## Learner trace and activity model

The Experience Overview exposes the system's observable units. Depending on the assignment, these include:

- verbatim patient-interaction transcript;
- subjective data collection;
- objective data collection;
- education and empathy;
- documentation;
- information processing;
- self-reflection;
- care plans and other assignment-specific activities;
- time-on-task;
- attempt status, turn-in, reopening, and remediation;
- SPI, proficiency level, activity scores, and DCE score.

Subjective findings can be defined as scored items that learners must uncover; objective assessment is scored for accuracy and completeness. Optional interview guides can reveal topics, progress, or even specific scored discoveries. This shows that the natural-language patient encounter sits inside a structured assessment model rather than an unconstrained conversation.

## Attempts and feedback

Repeated practice is configurable, not universal. Flexible Turn-in can allow multiple fresh attempts and let a student submit a preferred score. Reopen can allow a learner to return to a completed encounter and improve it. Both are bounded by faculty settings, due dates, and platform integration. Detailed feedback can also be delayed.

## Measurement semantics

The documentation distinguishes concepts that marketing often compresses together:

- **SPI** is described as a valid and reliable instrument for evaluating clinical judgment and critical-thinking performance on patient-exam assignments. It is based on points from observable activities and can compare performance with the level expected of a high-performing graduate/model exam.
- **DCE Score** is the percentage Elsevier recommends for grading. Where SPI is used, DCE Score normalizes performance against a national student average and standard deviation to a mean of 80%. Elsevier describes it as a fair assessment of student effort and allows threshold/pass-fail use in some clinical-replacement contexts.
- **Proficiency Level** is relative to representative student populations: Beginning is the bottom 50%, Developing the next 25%, and Proficient the top 25%.
- **Practice Readiness Dashboard** aggregates course, student, engagement, assignment, and category-strength/weakness data for faculty.

The public support documentation does not, on its own, establish one absolute cross-program threshold at which a learner is declared clinically practice-ready.
