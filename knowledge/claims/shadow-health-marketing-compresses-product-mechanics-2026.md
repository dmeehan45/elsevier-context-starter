# Shadow Health marketing compresses a configurable learning and assessment system into outcome language

## Scope

Public Elsevier/Shadow Health marketing and public product-support documentation available as of September 14, 2026.

## Evidence

Marketing repeatedly describes Shadow Health in terms of end-state educational outcomes: realistic patient encounters, clinical reasoning and judgment, confidence, measurable growth, and practice readiness. The 2026 brand expansion goes further, describing Shadow Health as a comprehensive patient-encounter solution across screen, lab, and VR modalities.

The support documentation exposes a more conditional mechanism. Faculty configure whether an experience is formative, summative, or hybrid; whether learners receive guides and immediate feedback; whether they can make fresh attempts or reopen work; and which activities are present. Learner performance is then decomposed into observable tasks such as subjective/objective data collection, education/empathy, information processing, documentation, and other assignment-specific activities.

The measurement layer is also more specific than the marketing shorthand. SPI is a task-derived performance measure; DCE Score is a normalized grading score; proficiency bands are peer-relative; and the Practice Readiness Dashboard aggregates course and student performance data. The public documentation reviewed does not define one universal absolute threshold that proves clinical practice readiness across programs.

## Notes

This is best treated as **semantic compression rather than evidence of misleading marketing**. Buyers reasonably care about readiness, judgment, confidence, and program outcomes, while product documentation must explain operational mechanics. The useful strategic question is which outcome claims have direct measurement validity, which are proxies, and which are downstream aspirations.

Several recurring translations should remain explicit:

- “practice readiness” → evidence from assignment performance, proficiency, engagement, and faculty analytics; not obviously a single absolute readiness construct;
- “accurately measure clinical reasoning” → SPI and activity-level traces provide structured evidence, but the exact construct validity and longitudinal sensitivity require the separate evidence base;
- “practice as often as needed” → possible under some faculty configurations, not a universal product rule;
- “natural/genuine conversation” → learners can use their own words, but the encounter is still evaluated through structured scored findings and activities;
- “improved patient outcomes” → a downstream educational aspiration; the product directly measures learner behavior and performance, not real-world patient outcomes.
