# Nursing student populations vary systematically by educational pathway

## Claim

There is no single representative U.S. nursing-student life stage. Educational pathway strongly predicts the distribution of learner age, prior education, licensure status, clinical experience and likely life constraints.

The clearest national evidence is the NLN's 2023 age-by-program data: 77.4% of generic BSN learners were under 25, compared with 38.5% of ADN learners and 17.4% of RN-to-BSN learners. Only 5.1% of doctoral learners were under 25, while 78.6% were older than 30.

Accelerated prelicensure learners add another distinct combination: they are clinically novice but already hold a prior bachelor's degree. RN-to-BSN learners are already licensed. Graduate advanced-practice learners are often experienced nurses becoming novices in a new scope.

## Implication

Learner segmentation and simulation design should not use one conventional undergraduate persona as the default for all nursing education. At minimum, the system should distinguish initial professional formation, licensed-nurse degree completion, career-change prelicensure, advanced-practice scope transition, and established-career leadership/research education.

## Guardrail

Pathway and demographics describe population concentrations, not individual capability. Personalization should rely primarily on demonstrated competence, prior professional experience and learner goals rather than inferring behavior from age, race, gender or family status.
