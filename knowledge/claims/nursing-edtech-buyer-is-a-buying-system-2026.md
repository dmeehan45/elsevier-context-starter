# Nursing educational technology is purchased by a buying system, not a single dean

## Claim

For consequential nursing educational technology, the roles of problem owner, curricular approver, nursing executive sponsor, budget holder, technology/privacy/accessibility approver, procurement/legal gatekeeper, implementation owner and renewal decision-maker are commonly distributed across multiple people or teams.

The nursing dean or nurse administrator is often a central actor because nursing accreditation standards place program leadership and fiscal-resource authority with that role. However, faculty governance and institutional technology/procurement controls constrain a model in which the dean can independently select and operationalize a product.

The distribution of decision rights becomes more pronounced as product scope, data sensitivity, integration depth, price and cross-program/campus reach increase.

## Evidence

- ACEN requires formal faculty and nurse-administrator participation in governance and gives the nurse administrator authority to lead the program, prepare the budget with faculty input and administer allocated fiscal resources.
- Public university software-purchasing policies demonstrate additional institutional gates for security, privacy, accessibility, architecture, finance, procurement and legal review.
- Large nursing-school organizational structures distribute academic-program, clinical-placement, learning-resource, simulation, finance and campus responsibilities across specialized leaders.

## Product implication

Shadow Health account research should model decision rights explicitly. A single CRM field such as 'buyer' or 'decision maker' is insufficient for predicting adoption, implementation quality, expansion and renewal.
