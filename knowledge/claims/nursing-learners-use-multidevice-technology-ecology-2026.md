# Nursing learners operate in a multi-device technology ecology centered on laptops and smartphones

## Claim

Available higher-education evidence, nursing-specific studies and current U.S. nursing-program technology requirements support a durable pattern:

- full Windows/macOS laptops are the primary academic workhorse and are commonly required by nursing programs;
- smartphones are the dominant companion device for communication, quick information access and some point-of-care workflows;
- tablets are meaningful but inconsistently required, accepted or provisioned;
- specialized simulation/VR/telehealth hardware is generally institution-provided rather than assumed learner-owned equipment.

Clinical settings form a separate technology context because personal mobile-device use may be limited by facility policy, privacy, professionalism and patient-care workflows.

## Scope and limitations

This claim concerns the **shape of the device ecology**, not exact national ownership percentages for U.S. nursing students. Current public data do not provide a sufficiently strong national nursing-specific estimate of Windows vs macOS share, device age/performance or tablet ownership.

## Product implication

For nursing education products, laptop/browser support remains the safest primary compatibility baseline while smartphone/tablet experiences should be designed around specific companion or context-appropriate jobs rather than an assumption that nursing learning is generically mobile-first.
