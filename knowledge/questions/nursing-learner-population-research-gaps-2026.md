# Nursing learner population — unresolved questions

Public national data gives us a good pathway/age/enrollment backbone, but several questions matter enough for Shadow Health that they should be validated with internal data, institution-level datasets or new primary research.

## Life conditions by pathway

1. What percentage of learners in ADN, generic BSN, accelerated BSN, RN-to-BSN, MSN and DNP programs work while enrolled, and how many hours?
2. How often do learners have dependent children, elder-care responsibilities or single-parent obligations by pathway?
3. How large are commute, parking, travel and childcare burdens created by clinical and simulation schedules?
4. Which learners depend on employer tuition support, family support, loans or continued paid employment to remain enrolled?
5. Which life constraints most strongly predict missed simulation, late completion, withdrawal or poor engagement?

## Prior experience

6. What proportion of prelicensure learners have prior healthcare employment such as CNA, medical assistant, EMT, military medic or allied-health work?
7. What occupations do second-degree learners come from, and which prior capabilities transfer into nursing education?
8. For graduate students, what are the distributions of years in practice, specialty and setting before re-entry to school?

## Career formation

9. When do learners form specialty preferences, and how stable are those preferences from admission through graduation and the first two years of practice?
10. Which clinical or simulation experiences cause learners to change specialty interest?
11. Are some patient populations systematically under-experienced because of local clinical-placement availability?
12. Can virtual simulation broaden career awareness without unintentionally steering learners toward or away from specialties based on scenario quality rather than real fit?

## Post-licensure return

13. What is the typical time from initial RN licensure to RN-to-BSN, NP, CRNA, DNP, leadership, education or PhD enrollment?
14. Which returns are primarily employer-driven, role-required, financially motivated, scope-seeking or responses to bedside/work-life strain?
15. How often does a nurse return to school while remaining with the same employer?
16. Which employers actively sponsor graduate education, and what obligations or retention agreements accompany support?
17. After completing graduate education, how often do nurses change role, setting, employer, specialty or compensation?

## Shadow Health internal questions

18. What do current learner profiles reveal about age, pathway, licensure, prior practice and setting across Shadow Health collections?
19. Do graduate learners complain about repetition of prelicensure-like tasks or insufficient recognition of existing expertise?
20. Do accelerated learners behave differently from traditional BSN learners in completion time, repetition, help-seeking or performance?
21. Which scenarios are disproportionately used for remediation versus initial instruction?
22. Can existing learner histories be followed across institutions or across prelicensure/postlicensure boundaries today?
23. What data would be educationally valuable to persist longitudinally, and which data should deliberately not be collected for privacy/equity reasons?

## Research priority

The highest-value next step is a paired quantitative/qualitative study using **existing Shadow Health learner/account data plus 20-30 learner interviews sampled across pathway transitions**. Public demographic research can tell us where concentrations probably exist; actual product behavior will tell us whether those differences matter to simulation design.
