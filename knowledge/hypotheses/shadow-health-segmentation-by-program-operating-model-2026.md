# Hypothesis: program operating model predicts Shadow Health value better than degree label alone

## Hypothesis

For simulation-product strategy, adoption and expansion, nursing programs are better segmented by a combination of:

- learner level/program type;
- regulatory treatment of simulation;
- clinical-placement scarcity;
- organizational topology;
- simulation maturity/infrastructure;
- delivery geography;
- faculty capacity;
- buying-system complexity;

than by degree label, institution prestige, or campus identity alone.

## Why this is plausible

Programs teaching similar BSN outcomes can differ substantially in:

- whether curriculum is centrally governed across campuses;
- which patient populations and specialties local clinical partners can expose learners to;
- how far students travel for simulation/clinical work;
- whether simulation is a dedicated school resource or a shared health-sciences service;
- who controls budget versus curriculum versus implementation;
- whether simulation can carry regulated practice-learning credit.

Those differences should affect both the educational job Shadow Health performs and the friction of adopting it.

## Predictions

If the hypothesis is correct:

1. Schools with high clinical-exposure variance should value standardized virtual-patient exposure more, all else equal.
2. Multi-campus systems with centralized curriculum should value cross-campus consistency and comparable learner evidence more than isolated course collections alone.
3. Programs with low simulation staffing/maturity should respond more strongly to implementation/debrief/remediation support than to modality breadth.
4. Mature simulation centers should demand deeper interoperability, analytics, configurability and evidence rather than basic access to scenarios.
5. Distributed/online programs should value virtual practice that reduces travel and bridges gaps between regional in-person experiences.
6. International markets where simulation cannot count toward required clinical hours should respond better to competency/readiness positioning than to 'clinical replacement' positioning.
7. Enterprise expansion should correlate with successfully mapping and serving the full buying system, not only dean-level sponsorship.

## How to test

Join product/CRM/account data with account-level attributes:

- institution/program archetype;
- campuses and centralization model;
- cohort size;
- simulation-center model;
- local clinical-placement constraints;
- regulator/simulation-credit rules;
- number and type of stakeholders in adoption;
- licensing/payment model;
- product collections adopted;
- activation/usage depth;
- faculty engagement/support burden;
- renewal/expansion history.

Then test whether these variables explain adoption depth, retention and expansion better than degree type and institution size alone.
