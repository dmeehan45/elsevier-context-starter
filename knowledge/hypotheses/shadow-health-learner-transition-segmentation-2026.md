# Hypothesis: learner transition is a stronger product-segmentation variable than degree label alone

## Hypothesis

For Shadow Health and adjacent nursing-learning products, the learner's **current professional transition** will predict useful simulation design, engagement and perceived relevance better than degree/program label alone.

Candidate transition states include:

- first professional formation;
- career-change entry into nursing;
- licensed-nurse credential completion;
- transition from student to practicing RN;
- specialty transition;
- transition from RN to advanced-practice scope;
- transition from clinician to systems/leadership/education/research role;
- return to clinical practice after time away or after graduate education.

## Why plausible

The same academic label can contain learners with materially different prior competence and life conditions. Conversely, learners in different programs can be solving the same professional transition.

A transition-based model would allow the product to adapt:

- scaffolding depth;
- scenario complexity;
- assumed vocabulary/workflow knowledge;
- remediation strategy;
- pacing and repetition;
- career-context framing;
- assessment of prior competence.

## Tests

1. Segment current Shadow Health learner/user data by program type and, where available, prior licensure/experience. Compare completion, repeat behavior, scores, support burden and satisfaction.
2. Interview faculty from generic BSN, accelerated, ADN, RN-to-BSN and advanced-practice programs about where current simulation feels mismatched to learner experience.
3. Interview learners about which activities feel redundant, appropriately challenging or unrealistically advanced.
4. Test whether a short intake/diagnostic capturing prior practice setting and experience improves simulation assignment recommendations beyond course/program metadata.
5. Examine whether transition-to-practice and post-licensure products can reuse the same competency evidence without forcing the nurse to start a new learner profile.

## Falsification conditions

The hypothesis weakens if course/program label already explains nearly all meaningful differences in learner behavior, if faculty deliberately require standardized identical experiences regardless of prior expertise, or if collecting transition-state information creates more complexity/privacy burden than educational value.
